\chapter{EVALUATION AND PRIVACY}

\section{Evaluation setup}

The system is evaluated at four levels: detection coverage, the per-student decision, the class estimate, and on-device performance. Several sources of evidence are used, because no single dataset has per-student attention labels for real lectures.

\textbf{System under test.} hcpose on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo-8 AI HAT (26 TOPS), Raspberry Pi OS trixie, HailoRT 4.23 / TAPPAS 5.1. The pose models are the project's own YOLOv8-pose upper-body models (13 keypoints) in three sizes, n, s and m, compiled to INT8 HEFs (the seeded compiles of chapter 3). Model weights were kept fixed throughout: all improvements are in pre- and post-processing and in the engagement logic.

\textbf{Metrics.} Coverage = share of labelled students matched to a detection (centre coverage ≥ 0.5 or IoU ≥ 0.3). Per-student: AUC, within-image AUC (only images with both classes, which removes the ``recognise the image'' shortcut), balanced accuracy, precision and recall of \emph{distracted}. Class level: mean class distracted \%, the width of its 95\% range, and inferred class size. Device: FPS, latency, CPU, memory, temperature, throttling, power.

Every number in this chapter comes from the INT8 HEFs running on the Pi's Hailo-8. Dataset poses come from m; the clips, the human labels and the staged session are scored with n, the deployment model, and with m.

\section{Detection coverage and tiling}

Without tiling, the model misses most students in the far rows; running it again on three zoomed-in tiles of the top 60\% of the frame lifts coverage with n from 54.6\% to 72.0\% of labelled students at threshold 0.3, and from 24.6\% to 65.5\% in the far third. With m it rises from 55.4\% to 75.1\%, and from 25.0\% to 69.4\% in the far third.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

\textbf{Method.} Besides the full frame, the model runs on one row of overlapping, roughly square tiles over the top 60\% of the image (three tiles at 1280×720). Tile detections are mapped back to the frame; those cut by an inner tile edge are dropped, and duplicates across passes are merged by IoU, or by containment plus agreeing keypoints. Both engines implement the same rules; the C++ merge in the GStreamer pipeline was checked equal to the Python merge on 25 random scenes. The detector weights are unchanged.

\textbf{On the deployed INT8 m model}, the number of matched SCB front-view students rose from 23,073 without tiles to 36,294 with them (+57\%); 13,446 students were found only with tiles, 225 only without.

\textbf{Cost.} Full frame plus three tiles is four inferences per frame, and the frame rate drops by exactly four: n from 131 to 32.8 FPS (still real time) and m from 31.5 to 7.9 FPS (enough for engagement, which needs 5-10 FPS, but not for a live camera without dropping frames). The tiles help most in dense rooms with far rows: in the study hall they find 2.3 times as many students with n, and 1.9 times with m. In the lecture clip, where the far rows are already large enough, they add only about two per frame.

\section{Duplicate boxes and the class estimate}

Two post-processing fixes made the class-level number more reliable: removing same-skeleton duplicate boxes, and counting only current, consistently seen seats. On the device, duplicates fell from 0.9-1.6 per frame to 0.00-0.03 on every clip and setting, and the inferred class size came close to the real head count, though it still overcounts the exam hall.

\textbf{Same-skeleton duplicates.} YOLO-pose often outputs a tight and a loose box for one student with the same skeleton. Their box IoU is low, so box NMS keeps both, and the analyzer then counts one student twice. After NMS, a box is dropped when it lies inside a stronger box (≥ 70\% of the smaller box) and at least three keypoints visible in both are, on average, closer than 0.1 × √(smaller box area). The same rule runs in the NumPy and the C++ post-process.

\textbf{Class estimate.} A plain share of \emph{detected} students is biased, because missed students are mostly in the far rows and not missed at random. The estimate therefore:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  remembers each seat's last state for \texttt{presence\_seconds} (15 s), so a student missed for a few frames still counts;
\item
  splits the seats into near, middle and far zones by image height, and weights each zone by its number of seats rather than by how many students happen to be detected there;
\item
  counts a seat toward the class size only if it was seen in the last 60 s and, before calibration, in at least 15\% of frames;
\item
  puts a 95\% range around the shown (smoothed) estimate.
\end{enumerate}

The range is a Wilson score interval \cite{b16} for a share p measured on n of N students. With a known class size N (\texttt{-\/-class-size}), it is narrowed by the finite-population correction, shrinking toward p so p stays inside:

\begin{equation}
\tilde p = \frac{p + \frac{z^2}{2n}}{1 + \frac{z^2}{n}}, \qquad h = \frac{z}{1 + \frac{z^2}{n}} \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n} + \frac{z^2}{4n^2}}, \qquad f = \sqrt{\frac{N - n}{N - 1}}
\end{equation}

with z = 1.96; the range is {[}\ensuremath{\tilde{p}} − h, \ensuremath{\tilde{p}} + h{]} clipped to {[}0, 1{]}, and with a known N its ends move toward p by the factor f.~Without a known N, no correction is applied: a size inferred from the seats seen so far is itself a lower bound, so counting every known seat is not a census. The range covers students who weren't counted, not classifier mistakes.

\textbf{Seat counting.} Before step 3, every box that persisted about 1.5 s became a permanent seat, and in the first version of the system the inferred class on the 6-minute study-hall clip grew to 78 for about 50 students. With the rule and n with tiles it is 59 there (39 with m), 26 on the lecture with 28 visible students, and 29 on the exam hall with 23 students and a teacher. The lecture is slightly undercounted; the exam hall is overcounted by about five. A teacher walking between the rows can leave seats along the way, which may explain part of that overcount, but this was not checked.

\textbf{Checks.} On the Pi, in all 208 analyses (every clip, model, tiling and decision), the shown estimate was never outside its range on any frame. Without \texttt{-\/-class-size} the rules' ranges are wide, 15-44 points, which is honest for samples that small.

\section{Per-student decision: rules versus learned models}

The learned classifiers beat the rules on SCB by a wide margin, but on real classes they call attentive students distracted, so the rules are the system's default and the learned models are an opt-in comparison.

\textbf{The two approaches.} The rules combine pose cues with a noisy-OR: head yaw beyond the seat's calibrated baseline, head dropped below it, face hidden while the shoulders are visible, torso turned, a hand at the face; a raised hand always counts as focused. Each seat learns its own attentive pose in its first 120 s of observation, and labels are smoothed (EMA with hysteresis). The learned models (gradient-boosted trees, logistic regression, an MLP 64-32) take the keypoints and the same cues as features and are trained on SCB students with the deployed INT8 m model's poses, with and without tiles. They run in NumPy on the Pi.

In the lecture clip, students sitting upright and looking at the board were labelled distracted, and students reading with their heads down focused (median head height above the shoulders 0.45 versus 0.33, n with tiles at 2:00). The reason is a gap in SCB's labels: its focused classes are read, write and hand-raising, all head-down or hand-up, while its distracted classes include turning the head and discussing, both head-up. SCB has no ``listening'' or ``looking at the board'' label, the most common attentive state in a lecture, so a model trained on it learns that a raised head means distracted. The learned models also depend strongly on pose quality: with n and no tiles, trees gave 26\% on the exam clip, against 9-11\% with m.

In the lecture clip with m and tiles, the rules gave 1.4\%, the trees 50.5\%, logistic regression 74.4\% and the MLP 53.8\%; with n, 2.1\%, 59.1\%, 82.3\% and 57.7\%.

Making a learned model usable would need training labels that include attentive, head-up students. The next section tests whether a public dataset with such a label is enough.

\section{A second dataset: ClaBehavior}

ClaBehavior \cite{b28} has the label SCB lacks: \emph{lookup}, a student with the head up looking at the board. It has 400 frames from 13 fixed cameras in Chinese K-12 classrooms, with 4,480 lookup boxes, 1,021 read, 1,011 turn\_head, 684 raise\_hand, 579 write, 242 discuss and 66 stand. Its images are treated like SCB's and like minors' data: they stay on the machines, and only poses, labels and numbers are used.

\textbf{Mappings.} Neither dataset labels attention, so two mappings from action to focused or distracted were fixed before training:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Strict:} read, write, raise hand and lookup are focused; turning the head is distracted. Bowed head (it looks like writing), discussing (it may be group work) and standing (it may be answering) are left out.
\item
  \textbf{Lenient:} as strict, but those three count as distracted.
\end{itemize}

The mapping used above for SCB alone counts bowed head and discussing as distracted. On SCB alone the strict mapping is easier: trees reach AUC 0.890 (within-image 0.861, balanced accuracy 80.3\%) and the MLP 0.903 (0.872, 82.3\%).

No threshold gave a clean set of views, so the coarsest grouping tried (41 groups) is the one quoted; from 85 groups to 41 the scores moved by at most 0.02. \section{Privacy by design}

The system never stores or transmits an image by default: each frame lives in memory for one inference, and the only default output is a class-level share shown on the teacher's screen.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/dataflow.png}
\caption{Data flow in hcpose with default settings; optional outputs are dashed}\label{fig:data-flow-in-hcpose-with-default-setting}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Frames} are decoded into RAM, passed to the Hailo-8 and freed. Nothing is written to disk unless \texttt{-\/-output} is set, which is meant for development and evaluation, not for classes.
\item
  \textbf{Skeletons} (13 keypoints and a box per person) carry no pixels. They identify nobody by themselves, but tied to a seat and a time in a known class they could point to a student, so they are treated as personal data and kept in memory.
\item
  \textbf{Seat states} use anonymous seat numbers that exist only for one session. There is no face recognition, no name list and no link between sessions, except an optional calibration file (\texttt{-\/-calibration}) that stores each seat's attentive pose and position, not identities.
\item
  \textbf{The class share} with its range is the only number meant to leave the program. It describes the class, not a student, and is not personal data.
\item
  \textbf{The screen} shows the camera image with labels during development. For classroom use, \texttt{-\/-display\ class} limits the overlay to the class number, and \texttt{-\/-headless} shows no window at all.
\item
  \textbf{Network:} hcpose opens no network connection; the device can run fully offline.
\end{itemize}

What a saved record holds, when \texttt{-\/-save-json} is used: one line per frame, from clip\_4 at 3:50, shortened to one person and three of the 13 keypoints. There are coordinates, scores and states, but no pixels and no identity. The seat number exists only within the session.

\begin{codeblock}
{"type": "frame", "t": 229.97, "w": 1280, "h": 720,
 "class": {"visible": 2, "counted": 2, "focused": 1, "distracted": 1,
           "class_pct_distracted": 50.0, "class_ci": [9.5, 90.5], "class_size": 2},
 "persons": [{"score": 0.75, "box": [663.9, 296.1, 863.6, 709.2],
              "keypoints": [[771.9, 356.1, 0.98], [776.5, 348.7, 0.91], [761.6, 349.6, 0.95], "…"],
              "engagement": {"seat": 2, "state": "focused", "score": 0.0, "reason": null, "calibrated": true}}]}
\end{codeblock}

Compared with a cloud service, nothing has to be uploaded, stored by a third party or transferred across borders, which removes the cross-border transfer obligations and most of the breach risk.

\section{Legal fit in Vietnam}

\section{Limitations}

The largest gap is ground truth: no result so far measures per-student accuracy on a real lecture, only on SCB actions or through the context of mostly attentive clips.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Proxy labels.} SCB labels actions (reading, turning, discussing), not attention, and comes mostly from Chinese K-12 classrooms. 2,891 SCB students carried both a focused and a distracted label across duplicate images (in the first version's count, mostly ``bowed head'' versus ``reading or writing''), so even the annotators could not always separate these. ClaBehavior adds the missing head-up class but is small: 400 frames from 13 cameras.
\item
  \textbf{Clip evidence is contextual.} The three clips have no labels; ``the true share is close to 0'' rests on what the class is doing. They show false alarms, not missed distraction. The human labels and the staged session address this.
\item
  \textbf{Few rooms, third-party footage.} Three third-party clips from three rooms, one of them with a camera outside the intended mounting, plus one small class recorded for this work. Results may not carry over to other layouts, lighting or age groups.
\item
  \textbf{Camera height.} With a camera at desk height, a head bowed over writing hides the face and reads as ``turned away'': the rules gave 55\% distracted in a study hall where everyone wrote (n with tiles). The design assumes a camera mounted high at the front; a lower \texttt{face\_hidden} weight (0.3) cuts that to 30\% for low cameras.
\item
  \textbf{Calibration time.} Each seat needs 120 s of usable observation. In a 6-minute dense clip only 16 of 59 seats finished calibrating, so short sessions run mostly on room-level baselines.
\item
  \textbf{Small classes, wide ranges.} Without the head count the rules' 95\% range spans 15-44 points on the clips. This is a property of the sample size, not a defect, but it limits how finely the indicator can be read.
\item
  \textbf{Hand-set rule weights.} The rule weights were set by design, not fitted, and were deliberately not tuned on the clips used to evaluate them; one documented change, the low-camera weight, is an option rather than the default.
\item
  \textbf{One compile per model.} INT8 compiles of the same weights differ by about ±0.01 AP; every result comes from one chosen compile per size. m with tiles also cannot keep up with a live 25-30 FPS camera without dropping frames.
\item
  \textbf{Staged session.} Adult volunteers acting scripted behaviour in a small room are easier to see and more exaggerated than real distraction, so its recall is an upper bound.
\item
  \textbf{Close cameras and full-body boxes.} In a small room filmed from close range, seats multiply (13-16 for 10 students in clip\_5), and a full-body box on a student whose legs are visible reads as a turned torso. Both come from geometry tied to the box: the seat anchor at its top and the box height in \texttt{body\_turned}. Keypoint-based anchors and scales would avoid them; they were not changed after clip\_5, to keep it a held-out test.
\end{itemize}
